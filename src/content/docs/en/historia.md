## How the collective house came about

The Rudbeckia collective house is the result of ten years of work. [The collective housing association](https://rudbeckiakollektivhus.se/) was founded in Uppsala in 2011 to bring a collective house to the city. Before that there was already, among others, [the Blenda collective house](https://www.uppsalahem.se/bostader/vara-omraden/blenda/) with Uppsalahem as landlord.

The original idea was a 40+ housing project without children — not senior housing, but aimed at older working people and pensioners. Over time the focus shifted to intergenerational living, open to every stage of life: children, students, young adults, families and pensioners – everyone who wants to live collectively.

## The collective house

[Sveafastigheter](https://sveafastigheterbostad.se/) (now Hemvist) developed the house together with Uppsala municipality and the collective housing association. [Belatchew Arkitekter](https://belatchew.com/projekt/rudbeckia/) designed this space-efficient home with generous shared areas. The house has seven floors; at the very top there is a glazed conservatory and a roof terrace with a view over Uppsala and the nature reserves around [Rosendal](./rosendal/).
